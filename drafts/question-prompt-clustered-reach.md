# Question prompt: which venues a clustered adopter set can see at all

Drafted 2026-10-11, not yet run. One of a set that looks for research questions the setting and
the logged literature support better than the current adoption-threshold question does. The
setting means every object, assumption and observation rule in
[`drafts/problem-statement-v2.md`](problem-statement-v2.md) except the paragraph beginning "Our
question is". The setting stays fixed. Only the question moves.

- **Goal:** find out whether the network-sampling literature supports a question of the form
  "which part of the venue set does a clustered adopter set reach, and which part stays
  structurally invisible" better than it supports a question about a critical adoption rate.
- **Context:** `AI/STATUS.md` records that the threshold literature disagrees on the direction
  of the clustering effect. `SRC-120` finds clustering raises the percolation threshold holding
  the degree distribution fixed, `SRC-121` finds it lowers the transition holding only mean
  degree fixed, and `SRC-122` gives a sign criterion that holds for one model and fails for
  others. A literature that disagrees on sign cannot support a claim about a critical rate. The
  same record calls `SRC-123` the closest real match of that round, because it measures sampling
  reach rather than a threshold: strong community structure capped recruitment near 85 percent
  of the population, and whole subgroups went unreached rather than mis-estimated.
- **Current understanding:** `SRC-123`, `SRC-113`, `SRC-114`, `SRC-115`, `SRC-116`, `SRC-125`
  and `SRC-126` are `CHECKED`. None of them maps a clustered set of people onto a set of places.
  The setting needs that second step, because an unreached social group matters here only when
  it is the group that fills a venue. `AI/STATUS.md` also records that no logged source reports
  an assortativity coefficient, an intraclass correlation, or a modularity value for an adopter
  set, and that none studies a location-based venue app. The working hypothesis, unverified, is
  that a reach question is answerable from structure alone, while a threshold question needs the
  parameter nobody has measured.
- **Desired deliverable:** read sources on the reach of a clustered or assortative subset of a
  network over a second mode, meaning places, events, groups or affiliations, rather than over
  the network itself. Bipartite and affiliation-network sampling are the expected areas. For each
  source state what it holds fixed and what it reports. Then state plainly whether a reach
  question is better supported than a threshold question, and whether the two-mode step exists
  in the literature or would be this project's own derivation.
- **Constraints:** treat the 45 `CHECKED` sources as the literature we hold. Open the relevant
  `LEAD` before asserting that a question is unanswered. Searching outward for new sources is
  part of the job, and is expected here, since the two-mode step is absent from the record. Do
  not invent citations. Mark a new source `LEAD` until read. Do not recommend which question the
  project should adopt, and do not treat clustered adoption as a chosen extension, since
  `AI/PROJECT.md` still records the extension slot as undecided. Do not edit the problem
  statement. A negative answer is a real answer; report it.
- **Definition of done:** a short list of read sources with pinpoints, a statement of whether
  two-mode reach under clustering has a quantitative treatment, and an explicit comparison of
  support against a threshold question.
