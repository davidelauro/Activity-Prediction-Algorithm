# Search prompt: how clustered is real app adoption on a social graph

Drafted 2026-10-11, not yet run. Companion to
`search-prompt-clustered-adoption-threshold.md`. That prompt asks for the theory connecting
clustering to a threshold; this one asks whether real app or product adoption is actually
clustered, and by how much, so any theoretical model has a real magnitude to use rather than an
assumed one.

- **Goal:** find empirical studies measuring how strongly product or app adoption clusters
  along a social network, ideally with a usable number (an assortativity coefficient, a
  friend-adoption correlation, a referral or viral coefficient, or a measured intraclass
  correlation of adoption within friend groups).
- **Context:** this project currently treats adoption rate as a single aggregate percentage
  with no stated selection mechanism. Whether adoption clusters strongly or weakly changes how
  much the threshold-shift question (the companion prompt) actually matters in practice for this
  project's own scale (about 30,000 members, 75 to 125 venues).
- **Current understanding:** none. This is a pure literature gap right now; the owner's claim
  that adoption clusters is an informed hunch about how apps typically spread, not a cited fact.
- **Desired deliverable:** sources read directly that measure real adoption or install patterns
  against a social graph, ideally from a social, dating, or location-based app rather than a
  generic product, since adoption mechanics vary by category. Ideally a usable number, not only
  a qualitative claim that network effects exist.
- **Constraints:** do not invent citations. Mark results `LEAD` until personally read. This
  feeds a candidate extension, not yet chosen (see `AI/PROJECT.md`), so nothing here commits the
  project to using it.
- **Definition of done:** a verified, numeric sense of how clustered real app adoption tends to
  be, or an honest statement that no such number exists in the literature, the same honest
  outcome the earlier posting-probability search reached.
